The problem in two facts

The official booklet is 88 pages. Most people read the headlines instead.

And the headlines disagree. On Question 9, one outlet wrote that a YES vote repeals the gun law. The Secretary of State says a YES vote keeps the law. A voter who reads only the news can walk in and vote the opposite of what they meant.

The 30-second version
Step 1

You answer 20 seconds of questions

Town. Rent or own. A few one-sentence checkboxes like "I own a gun" or "I've used a public defender." Nothing leaves your browser.

Step 2

Your ballot re-sorts itself

Questions that touch you rise to the top with a yellow badge. Questions that apply to everyone sit in the middle. The rest collapse.

Step 3

Every claim has a receipt

Each sentence carries a chip. Tap it and the exact Attorney General paragraph opens. We never paraphrase and never say how to vote.

Where the AI goes

Two features, both grounded in the booklet

Ask the official text

A box on each card. The model gets only that question's Attorney General summary and full text, and must cite a paragraph in every sentence. Ask "how should I vote" and it points you to the booklet.

Draft a message to your election office

80 words, built from the questions you flagged and their citations. You edit and copy it. The app never sends anything, and your profile never leaves the browser.

The relevance sorting itself is not AI. It is a small rules table we write and review by hand, so it runs instantly in the browser and never hallucinates.
